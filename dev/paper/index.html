    HRAgents: an auditable HR agent platform for the department of one¶

    Davin Akmal Yasha · v0.2.0 · Apache-2.0

    Abstract¶

    Small organisations do not have an HR department; they have one person who carries hiring, onboarding, records, leave, payroll preparation, compliance, growth and offboarding, usually alongside another job. LLM agents can plausibly absorb the reading and drafting, but they cannot be trusted with the decisions, and a system that quietly guesses at a rejection is worse than the backlog it replaces. HRAgents is a self-hosted platform built around one division: agents extract and draft, deterministic code scores and routes, a named human decides. Every state transition is written to a hash-chained audit log, every score cites the evidence behind it, and every automation boundary is expressed as a pure function with a test. This paper describes the design, the measured behaviour, and the limitations that remain.

    1. The problem¶

    A solo HR person is asked to make high-volume, high-consequence decisions with no second reader. Three failure modes follow:

    1. Silent rejection. A CV is skimmed for ten seconds; the candidate never hears back. At scale this is both a fairness problem and a reputational one.
    2. Inconsistent judgement. The same profile reads differently on a Monday than on a Friday, and nobody can reconstruct why a decision was made.
    3. Unverifiable automation. Tools that "use AI for HR" typically hide a model call behind a score. The score cannot be inspected, reproduced, or defended.

    The regulatory direction is unambiguous: under Indonesia's UU PDP and the EU AI Act's transparency expectations, a decision about a person needs a record of who decided, on what basis, and with what human involvement.

    2. Design principles¶

    Extract, don't decide. Agents turn unstructured documents into a typed CandidateProfile with provenance references. They can also draft a summary or a feedback note. They never produce the number that gates a person, and an unparseable document degrades to a human review rather than an error.

    Evidence first. Every score dimension carries the references that produced it. A dimension with no reference contributes zero, even if the title says "Senior". The pairing rule matters: missing evidence also blocks automatic rejection, because an extraction gap is not a property of the candidate.

    Named humans gate outcomes. Rejections above the merit floor require a person with the authority the approval engine checks, a reason, and an audit entry. Payroll is prepared, verified and exported; payment execution is out of scope. There are no shell tools, ever.

    Operator data, not hardcoded law. Statutory rates, tax brackets and overtime premiums arrive as empty, unverified tables. A named human enters the numbers, records the source, and certifies them; payroll refuses to compute otherwise. Rubric thresholds travel in a verified rubric version, so changing a band is the same act as changing a rate.

    Make the record the product. The audit chain is append-only and tamper-evident. Evaluations persist the rubric family and content digest that produced them. A reviewed payroll run is immutable; a re-run supersedes it with an explicit link instead of rewriting history.

    3. System¶

    flowchart LR
    IN[Documents · forms · email] --> Q[(Queue)]
    Q --> W[Worker]
    W --> A[Extraction agents]
    A -->|typed profile + refs| S[Deterministic scorer]
    S -->|four dimensions + evidence| P[Policy engine]
    P -->|auto-schedule| C[Scheduling]
    P -->|HITL| H[Named human]
    H -->|approve · reject · override| AU[(Audit chain)]
    C --> AU
    H --> D[Leave · Payroll · Growth · Compliance · Offboarding]
    D --> AU

    The API is FastAPI over a sync domain layer (ADR 0005). PostgreSQL carries the tenant-scoped tables under row-level security; Redis carries the evaluation queue; the dashboard is a React PWA served by the same process. K workers claim messages, run k independent extractions, and the scorer produces one score tensor per run; variance across runs (σ) is a first-class input to the policy.

    4. Scoring and calibration¶

    Four dimensions make the tensor: technical depth, stack alignment, systems literacy and verifiable certifications. Each family (engineering, finance, education, healthcare, legal, operations, sales, general) supplies its own breadth vocabulary, professional signals, title tiers and weight mix, exposed as one taxonomy object; the dimensions compose from a small set of pure primitives (saturation, evidence density/count, credential credit, title tiers). The engineering template's digest and serialized score vector are pinned as regression fences.

    Routing is a pure function over S, σ, evidence sufficiency, anomaly flags, calendar constraints and consent:

    Condition Decision
    consent inactive halt, human review
    anomaly flags human review (HITL_ANOMALY)
    no mutual slots human review (HITL_CALENDAR)
    S ≥ 0.85 ∧ σ ≤ 0.05 ∧ evidence AUTO_SCHEDULE
    S ≥ 0.85 ∧ σ ≤ 0.05 ∧ ¬evidence HITL_MANUAL
    ¬evidence (any other score) HITL_MANUAL
    0.70 ≤ S < 0.85 ∧ evidence soft-rejection queue, sign-off required
    S < 0.70 ∧ evidence documented rejection with feedback

    Calibration is label-free: the corpus asserts orderings (strong > adequate > weak, matched fit > tenured misfit) rather than invented ground truth, because a ground-truth label would let anyone move a number until a test passed.

    5. Evaluation¶

    Corpus. 50 synthetic profiles — 18 engineering and 32 non-IT (finance, operations, sales, admin, customer support), English and Bahasa Indonesia, in Markdown, JSON and a real PDF — plus 15 edge cases: date overlaps, career changes, overqualification, freelance history, a fake certificate, a scanned image-only PDF, an oversized document, a malformed JSON export, two prompt-injection CVs and a ghost-job mismatch. Four name-swap pairs test scorer invariance.

    Offline suite. Runs in CI with the deterministic test model, no network: structural and safety assertions pass 100% across the five base cases.

    Live run. Five cases through xiaomi/mimo-v2.5 (Command Code, ZDR enforced): 5/5, 100% of assertions, mean 81.7 s (21–152 s). Prompt injection is blocked and redacted; zero-width characters are stripped before the model. Latency is acceptable for the asynchronous pipeline and not for interactive use, which is why the dashboard shows progress rather than hiding it.

    Throughput. The ingestion path sustains 608.5 req/s = 36,509 req/min at p50 93 ms / p99 169 ms with zero errors in a 5,000-request run, and the audit chain verifies afterwards. This isolates application overhead; Postgres and Redis add deployment latency.

    6. Safety and security¶

    • Prompt injection is treated as untrusted content: suspected instructions are redacted and recorded, and no extracted contact can override a form value.
    • RBAC + RLS. Roles hold named permissions; the database enforces tenant isolation; keys can be bound to a role and an employee.
    • Secrets are operator-supplied. Provider credentials entered in the dashboard are Fernet-sealed at rest, masked to four characters in every response, and never written to the audit payload.
    • Immutability. Payroll runs are editable only while draft/assembling; a reviewed run is terminal and a re-run is a linked successor.
    • Uploads. An optional ClamAV scan runs before a document is stored: a clean file is recorded as scanned, an infected one is refused with the signature on the chain, and a configured-but-unreachable scanner refuses the upload instead of silently storing it unscanned.
    • Retention. Erasure walks a ledger with per-entity handlers; a failing handler is isolated and retried rather than silently skipped, payroll is pseudonymized, and the sweep reports what it destroyed, spared and failed. Each attempt is journaled before it runs, so a crash is reconciled and retried on the next pass instead of disappearing.

    7. Limitations¶

    Honest, current, and tracked in the repository:

    1. Live-model fairness is unproven. The harness compares name-swapped CV pairs by a decision signature (skills, experience, education, certifications, scored vector) and fails a live run on divergence; CI exercises the wiring offline, where the model is deterministic and cannot show bias.
    2. Credential registries are operator imports. The registry exists, is imported and verified in the dashboard, and gates scoring once verified. There is no issuer API integration: an install that imports nothing treats every claim as before.
    3. Role fit is a coverage gate, not a model. Tenure-driven depth with thin must-have coverage now routes to a person, but the check is token coverage of the job's requirements; a richer per-family sufficiency model is future work.
    4. Cost is tokens, not currency. Each evaluation records its input/output tokens by provider and model, exposed per model; dollar cost is left to the operator's contract, and no cost-per-hire figure is claimed.
    5. The portfolio agent is not in the pipeline. Repository and publication evidence are implemented but the agent that gathers them is not wired into the production path.
    6. The offline model is not a quality signal. Its numbers are fake by construction; it exists to exercise the pipeline without a network.

    8. Availability¶

    Apache-2.0. Source, architecture decisions, deployment runbooks and the rendered documentation are at https://davinakmalyasha.github.io/HRAgents/; releases are tagged hr-agents-vX.Y.Z with a wheel, an sdist, a scanned multi-arch container image and an SBOM.
